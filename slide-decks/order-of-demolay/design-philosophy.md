# Heraldic Constellation

This visual language treats youth leadership as a constellation: individual lives become brighter when they are connected by purpose, friendship, and service. The composition should feel intentional and architectural, using a deep midnight field as the stable ground and allowing each idea to appear as a distinct illuminated node.

Heraldic geometry gives the work its identity. The official DeMolay International emblem, stars, rays, and measured borders carry the visual authority; supporting forms should echo the emblem without inventing a competing identity. The deck should feel like a contemporary chapter presentation rather than a collection of decorative clip art.

The official palette leads: Oxford Blue and its shade establish trust and structure; Crown Gold marks invitation, principle, and action; Sapphire Blue, Ruby Red, Beacon Orange, and Emerald Green provide controlled secondary emphasis. Color is therefore an information system, not surface decoration. Every accent must have a job and every empty area must provide visual breathing room.

Typography should be confident, sparse, and editorial. Bebas Neue serves as the approved free alternative for display headlines; Lato carries readable body copy; Crimson Pro adds ceremonial emphasis where appropriate. Large statements carry the emotional arc; short labels and compact supporting lines provide orientation. The slides should feel meticulously crafted, with deliberate alignment, controlled rhythm, and enough restraint that the visual system remains memorable after the words are gone.

The final work should look patiently made by a designer who understands both ceremony and youth culture: precise enough to honor the Order, alive enough to invite a new member in. Craftsmanship is visible in the spacing, the hierarchy, the repeated motifs, and the quiet consistency of every detail.
