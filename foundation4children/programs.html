Our Educator Training Program

A highly professional, intensive three-day workshop that equips Arizona educators to identify, support, and intervene early with students at risk.

What the program is

Our program is a highly professional, intensive three-day training workshop for Arizona educators — to learn, or improve their ability, to identify, intervene with, and create appropriate intervention plans for students that may be at risk.

This workshop trains a core team of four to eight educators from a school in how to intervene early and effectively with students who display patterns of behavior that threaten their success at school and in life.

The program involves practical practice sessions designed to simulate real events, along with presentations on the topics that matter most for student well-being.

Participants leave the training with a specific plan for behavioral interventions and a system for implementing student support in their schools — or incorporating worthwhile concepts and practices into their current program.

History & results

Measures of success

In 2010, the Arizona Masonic Foundation for Children surveyed participating educators from Arizona schools. Their findings:

Arizona schools, 2010

  • Of the 90.9% of students accepted into school intervention programs, 47.69% had completed the program at that time.
  • Of the closed intervention programs, 88.7% of children were showing a positive change.
  • Reasons for referral (sometimes multiple): Academic 49.2%, Behavioral 45%, Attendance 9%, Health 5%, Multiple Reasons 16%.

Research that backs the model

In November 2003, Dr. Carl Fertman of the University of Pittsburgh, with the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, published research on Student Assistance Programs in Pennsylvania schools — the first state to adopt this training model, in 1984.

The conclusion: these programs work. "We can emphatically report that Student Assistance Programs positively contribute to student academic success and achievement… and help to create healthy youth and a healthy future America."
