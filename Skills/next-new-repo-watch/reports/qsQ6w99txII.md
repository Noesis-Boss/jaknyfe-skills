## Description

What happens when you build an innovative AI product, only to see its features absorbed by ChatGPT and Claude? In this interview, Andrew Warner sits down with Nathan Baschez, founder of Lex, to discuss why the AI writing tool is winding down and what founders can learn from its journey.

Lex started as a better writing experience: a place where people could write while using AI as a thought partner, sparring partner, and idea generator. But as general-purpose AI tools added similar capabilities, competing as a standalone writing product became increasingly difficult.

Nathan shares why helping people improve their writing can be a tougher business than helping people build things they couldn't create before. He and Andrew discuss choosing the right market, building for enthusiastic AI users, and the challenges of creating a sustainable AI business in a rapidly changing landscape.

Most importantly, Nathan explains why founders should pursue what genuinely excites them instead of blindly following conventional business advice. His perspective is a reminder that even when a startup doesn't become a lasting independent business, the experience, lessons, relationships, and judgment gained along the way can be invaluable.
