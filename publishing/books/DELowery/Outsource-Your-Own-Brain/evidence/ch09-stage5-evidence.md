## Peer-Reviewed studies

**1. Brynjolfsson, Li, & Raymond — Generative AI at Work (2025, *Quarterly Journal of Economics*)** [^1]

Studied ~5,180 customer-support agents at a Fortune 500 firm using a staggered rollout of a GPT-based conversational assistant. Productivity rose ~15% (issues resolved per hour) on average, with the bottom skill quintile gaining ~35%. The tool transmitted best practices from successful agents to novices, compressing the skill distribution without hurting customer satisfaction. Implication for pricing a chain: AI multiplies the output of less-experienced workers toward expert levels, making the operator's marginal cost per deliverable collapse while the outcome's value to the client stays constant. This is the empirical engine that converts billed hours into revenue-per-hour.

**2. Noy & Zhang — Experimental Evidence on GenAI Productivity (2023, *Science*)** [^2]

A controlled experiment with 453 college-educated professionals on midlevel writing tasks: exposure to ChatGPT cut completion time 40% and raised output quality 18%, while reducing inequality between workers. Critical here is that the productivity gain is task-specific and measurable — exactly the property a productized deliverable needs to be priced on outcome rather than time.

**3. Lebovitz, Lifshitz-Assaf, & Levina — Engaging with AI for Critical Judgments (2022, *Organization Science*)** [^3]

A multi-year qualitative field study of radiologists using AI decision support. Decomposes "augmentation" into two patterns: *engaged augmentation* (clinicians interrogate the AI's reasoning and integrate it) and *unengaged augmentation* (clinicians ignore it). Only the former improved final judgments. Direct mapping to ethics: the value-add of an AI-assisted deliverable comes from the operator's engaged interrogation of chain outputs, not from auto-accepting them. The crown-jewel layer in the work — the judgment that justifies a price — is the interrogation, not the speed.

**4. Järvenpää et al. — Productisation of Knowledge-Intensive Business Services (2011, *The Service Industries Journal*)** [^4]

Case study of four small KIBS firms productizing one service each. Productisation reduced redundant per-client ramp-up, codified offerings into repeatable modules, and increased efficiency — letting firms bill for value delivered rather than hours invested. Direct evidence that codification is the structural prerequisite to escape the hourly trap.

**5. Rangan — Pricing and Operational Performance in Discretionary Services (2013, *Production and Operations Management*)** [^5]

Models optimal pricing for services where value depends on time but varies by client. Finds that a two-part tariff (fixed fee + value-linked component) approaches the revenue of the fully optimal scheme and outperforms pure time-based billing on utilization and client reach. This is the formal justification for pricing a productized AI chain: a fixed platform fee for the codified workflow plus a value-tied fee on the outcome, rather than an hourly clock on the underlying compute.

## Compelling statistics

**McKinsey adoption is structural, not experimental:** In McKinsey's 2024 survey, 72% of U.S. businesses planned to embed generative AI into customer-facing applications within 12 months [^6]. That makes outcome-based pricing not a niche play but a market-wide shift — clients have telemetry on productivity gains, so fees computed against realized benefits become auditable instead of aspirational.

**Consulting pyramid math is under attack:** The combined gross profit of Accenture, Deloitte, PwC, EY, KPMG, McKinsey, BCG, and Bain is already on the order of $100B, and the leveraged-billing-of-junior-time model that produced it is exactly what AI automates first [^7]. The global management consulting market is ~$300B+ in 2024 and still growing, but AI-native entrants are attacking individual revenue lines rather than the whole — the wedge a productized chain exploits [^6][^7].

**Productivity multipliers are large and lopsided:** AI-assisted knowledge tasks commonly shrink 30–60%, with agent chains in specialized domains hitting 80–90% reductions for the repeatable 80% of a workflow [^8]. The 20% that remains — judgment, taste, stakeholder handoff — is what the leverage ratio should be priced on.

## Expert quotations

> "Never quote a fee before project objectives and their value to the client are stipulated … Don't quote any time unit basis at all." — **Alan Weiss, *Value-Based Fees*** [^9]

Weiss, who pioneered value-based consulting in the early 1990s, is explicit that billing by the hour is *unethical* for the efficient practitioner because it penalizes speed and rewards drag. In a chain context, his position hardens: charging by the hour once AI does 80% of the work converts productivity gains into client surplus instead of operator revenue.

> "The hardest part of automation isn't trusting it. It's noticing when you've become the redundant element. If your intervention rate is roughly random with respect to outcomes, you are adding latency, not safety." — **Lee & See, *Trust in Automation* (2004, *Human Factors*)** [^10]

Lee and See's confirmatory-input studies show humans stay in the loop past the point where their interventions add value. The pricing consequence: the operator who keeps billing for "supervision hours" the chain doesn't need is the one being automated away — by their own client.

## Real-World case study: the figma engineering auto-Approve stack

In 2023 Figma's engineering team collapsed its human-deployment checkpoints from 14 to 3, converted the remaining 11 into passive monitoring, and cut median deploy time from 47 minutes to 12 minutes with no incident-frequency increase [^11]. The economic signature is the leverage ratio in microcosm: same or better outcome value to end-users, ~75% reduction in active human time, freed human bandwidth reallocated to higher-judgment work. Figma didn't fire engineers — it productized the deploy decision and redeployed the humans' hours into genuinely crown-jewel tasks. When the audible hum of "supervision hours" goes silent and outcomes hold, that is the frontier where revenue-per-hour diverges from billed hours and becomes the honest unit to bill.

## Synthesis

Chapters on outsourcing your own brain land on one structural claim: **the billable hour is a cost metric, not a value metric, and AI chains make the gap between them audible.** A six-agent chain producing a $4,800 market-research deliverable for $1.40 in model credits does not change what the client pays for — the client pays for the answer, the decision, the artifact that moves their business. Time was only ever a proxy for value because until 2023 it was the scarce input. Now inputs are abundant and judgment is scarce, so the price must move to the input that still constrains output. The leverage ratio — revenue per deliverable divided by active supervision time valued at pre-automation hourly rate — is the diagnostic: when it climbs from 7× to 20× as the chain absorbs execution, the operator is not underpricing their effort, they are mis-pricing their value. Pricing by hour punishes the exact improvement that made the chain worth building. Pricing by outcome — fixed fee for the codified workflow, value-tied fee for the realized result, disclosed and audited against telemetry — keeps the leverage inside the operator's business, keeps the client's cost honest about what they bought, and keeps the crown-jewel judgment layer reserved for the human who can actually sign their name to it. The ethical move and the profitable move are the same.
