# faceless-yt-retention-script

Use this skill as **stage 4** of the faceless-youtube-system. Stages: niche-brand → content-engine → discoverability → retention-script → monetization.

This stage consumes a single video idea from the calendar + the packaging from stage 3, and produces the time-coded script that gets filmed. The default viewer journey is **HOOK → OPEN LOOP → CONTEXT → VALUE → ESCALATION → PAYOFF → CTA**. Treat the title/thumbnail promise as a contract: every open loop needs a deliberate closure.

## Required input

- **Video idea** (one) from `Skills/faceless-yt-content-engine/`'s calendar — title, hook, primary keyword, viewer intent, pillar.
- **Niche + audience + tone** from `Skills/faceless-yt-niche-brand/`.
- **Packaging** (3 title variations + curiosity gap + main promise) from `Skills/faceless-yt-discoverability/`.
- **Target length** (the format sweet spot from stage 1).
- **Video type and tone** — educational, story, tutorial, commentary, or hybrid; conversational, authoritative, urgent, calm, etc.
- **Main goal** — watch time, returning viewers, subscribers, leads, or sales.

## Output (single markdown document per video)

### Section 1 — Reusable time-coded script framework

The framework is the same shape for every video; only the content changes. Fill each phase with the specific beats this video needs.

#### Phase 1 — 0–15 seconds: hook + open

Three responsibilities, in order:

1. **3–5s hook** (the opening line the viewer hears) — must do one of: name the specific payoff, interrupt the scroll, or pose the curiosity gap. A channel intro ("Hey guys, welcome back to the channel") eats this phase.
2. **Curiosity-driven opening** (5–10s) — the first sentence after the hook. Must convert the scrolled-past viewer into a viewer who keeps watching. Open a loop, set stakes, or deliver the first small payoff. **Do not reveal the video's full payoff here.**
3. **First pattern change** (~15s) — the first visual/audio change that re-grabs a viewer whose attention has slipped. Without this, the 0–15s drop-off in retention graphs is unrecoverable.

A common first 15 seconds: payoff line (3s) → tease first finding (7s) → title-card text + new visual (15s).

#### Phase 2 — 15–60 seconds: establish + justify

1. **Why this video exists** — the one sentence that justifies the viewer's time investment. Ties to the main promise from stage 3.
2. **What the viewer will walk away with** — the concrete thing. Not "you'll learn about X"; "you'll know the one filter that decides whether to take this trade."
3. **First small payoff** — a 10-second micro-reward that confirms the video is worth continuing. Without it, the 30–60s slope is the death zone for retention.
4. **Concrete support** — use an example, comparison, short story, or demonstration whenever it explains the point better than abstraction.

#### Phase 3 — 1–3 minutes: deep payoff

For 8–12 min videos, this is where the bulk of the value lands. Structure:

- **First major beat** (the headline point of the video).
- **Second major beat** (the supporting or contrasting point).
- **The contrarian beat** (something the niche mainstream gets wrong) — drives comments.
- **First payoff moment** — explicit "now you know X" or "this is the part most videos skip" beat. Marks the moment the viewer has received the main value.

#### Phase 4 — Middle section

For 8–12 min videos, this is the second half. Must contain:

- **A second pattern change** (visual re-set or section break) — re-energizes the viewer who has been watching 6+ minutes.
- **One specific example or case study** — abstraction is retention death; specifics hold attention.
- **A second curiosity loop** that closes a loop opened in Phase 1 or 2 (do not open loops you do not close).
- **An escalation beat** — raise the stakes, introduce a surprising implication, or show the cost of getting the idea wrong.
- **Planned pattern interrupts** — vary visual scale, motion, evidence type, sound, or on-screen treatment at natural attention dips. Each interrupt must add meaning.

#### Phase 5 — Final 30 seconds: payoff + CTA

1. **Second payoff moment** (the viewer gets the second of two things — first was in Phase 3, this is the closer).
2. **Loop closure** — explicitly answer the question the title/thumbnail posed. Do not leave the viewer to assemble the answer.
3. **Natural CTA** — the call-to-action tied to what the viewer just received, not bolted on. "If this saved you an hour, the next one saves you three" lands; "smash like and subscribe" bounces.
4. **Callback ending** — connect the final line or image back to the opening hook, then point to the next useful video or action.

The CTA must be a **soft mid-roll and a strong end**, not a single ask. For 8–12 min, place a non-intrusive mid-roll at 60–70% mark (often a "watch this next" rather than "subscribe").

### Section 2 — 5 fill-in-the-blank hook formulas

Each formula: structure + 2 worked examples + 1 anti-example (what the formula is **not** for).

1. **Specific outcome** — "In the next [N] minutes, you'll know [specific outcome] without [common pain]."
2. **Open loop** — "There's a [obscure thing] [audience] don't know about [topic], and it changes [outcome]."
3. **Pattern interrupt** — "[Common advice] is wrong. Here's what works instead."
4. **Numbered promise with a twist** — "[N] [things] that [outcome]. #3 is the one most people miss."
5. **Stakes opener** — "If you [common action] without [missing step], you'll [specific bad outcome]."

Fill in the slots, never the structure. Re-test each filled hook against the audience profile from stage 1.

### Section 3 — 5 reusable transition formulas

Each formula: structure + when to use + when **not** to use.

1. **The callback** — "Earlier I said [X]. Now let me show you what that means in practice." (Use when re-entering a topic after a digression.)
2. **The pivot** — "That's the theory. Here's what happens when [specific scenario]." (Use between explanation and example.)
3. **The countdown** — "Three things change when you do this. First…" (Use when delivering a list; gives the viewer a count to hold onto.)
4. **The reset** — "Stop. Before we go further, here's the one thing you must remember." (Use mid-video to re-anchor attention.)
5. **The tease-forward** — "And that — that's the setup for what happens at [later point]. Stay with me." (Use to bridge a 60–90 second gap between major beats.)

### Section 4 — Script delivery rules (anti-patterns to avoid)

- **No clickbait the video cannot deliver.** The title/thumbnail opened a loop; the script must close it. If the video does not actually answer the title's question, the loop is a betrayal and retention dies at 30s.
- **No "Hey guys, welcome back."** First 3 seconds are too valuable for channel housekeeping.
- **No mid-video "smash like" CTAs.** A single soft mid-roll, plus a strong end-roll. Nothing in between.
- **No tonal whiplash.** The opening tone must match the final-30s tone. Calm + specific throughout, or energetic + entertaining throughout.
- **No "we'll get to that later" without naming when.** "We'll cover that in 4 minutes" lands; "I'll explain later" loses the viewer.

## Quality rules

- Every phase must have a named responsibility, not just a duration. "0–15s: stuff happens" is not a framework.
- The two payoff moments (Phase 3 and Phase 5) must be the two strongest moments in the video. Do not save the best for last and have nothing in the middle.
- Hook formulas must be **tested against the audience definition** before being filled in. A hook that works for finance novices does not work for finance pros.
- Include a **retention audit**: identify the three strongest retention moments, the viewer question or emotion each creates, and why each earns continued watching.
- Mark every open loop with its planned closure. Remove vague "we'll get to that" promises.
