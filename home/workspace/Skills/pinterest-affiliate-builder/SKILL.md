# Pinterest Affiliate Builder

Build one complete Pinterest content campaign at a time. Optimize for useful search content, fresh creative, accurate links, and transparent affiliate disclosure—not spam, fabricated search volume, or guaranteed income.

## Required inputs

Ask for only missing inputs:

- Niche and audience
- Trending Pinterest search/topic, or permission to research one
- Article destination URL, affiliate product/link, or both
- Relevant boards and brand/style preferences

If the user has not chosen a niche, recommend one focused niche and explain the choice briefly. Do not scatter a campaign across unrelated niches.

## Workflow

### 1. Set up right

Confirm that the user has, or plans to create, a free Pinterest Business account. Explain that one focused niche makes testing and board organization easier. Do not imply that an account guarantees reach, analytics access, or income.

### 2. Find the trend

If current research is requested, use Pinterest Trends or another current first-party/credible source. Record:

- Exact search phrase
- Geographic/time filter used
- Whether the signal is rising, seasonal, or stable
- Evidence of competition or saturation
- Why the topic fits the niche

Never invent search volume, trend direction, or difficulty. If Pinterest Trends cannot be accessed, say so and label the result as a hypothesis that needs user validation.

### 3. Validate the keyword

Use this prompt internally or provide it to the user:

> I’m targeting the Pinterest search "[trending topic]" in the [niche] space. Give me: the core keyword, 3 secondary keywords with rough search difficulty (low/medium/high), and 2 content angles that aren’t already overdone for this topic. Explain the evidence and label difficulty as an estimate, not a measured score.

Return a compact keyword brief with the primary keyword, secondary keywords, intent, content gap, and a recommended angle.

### 4. Write the content

Create a genuinely useful article, normally about 1,200 words unless the user specifies another length. Use this prompt:

> Write a 1,200-word article on [trending topic]. Structure: hook intro, H2/H3 outline, an FAQ with 4 real searcher questions, and one natural spot to place [affiliate link/product]. Useful and specific—not generic AI filler. Include practical steps, concrete examples, limitations, and a clear distinction between firsthand claims, sourced facts, and recommendations.

The article must:

- Lead with the reader’s problem and search intent.
- Use the primary keyword naturally; avoid keyword stuffing.
- Include a descriptive title, meta description, suggested slug, and internal-link ideas when useful.
- Put affiliate disclosure near the relevant recommendation and include a clear site-wide disclosure suggestion.
- Avoid medical, financial, legal, or safety claims unless properly sourced and appropriately qualified.

### 5. Write the pin batch

Use this prompt:

> Write 5 Pinterest pin titles and descriptions for the article above. Titles under 100 characters, keyword-first, with a curiosity hook. Descriptions lead with the target keyword naturally in the first sentence. Make each pin materially different in promise, angle, and visual concept. Include the destination URL, board suggestion, alt text, and `#affiliate` or `#ad` when the pin is commercial.

Return a table with five rows. Keep titles under 100 characters and descriptions within Pinterest’s current limits after checking current platform guidance. Do not shorten or cloak URLs. Use the full destination URL supplied by the user.

### 6. Design and publish

For each pin, provide a visual brief:

- 2:3 vertical composition, normally 1000×1500 px unless current guidance says otherwise
- On-image headline matching the pin promise
- Visual subject, hierarchy, contrast, and brand treatment
- Alt text
- Full destination URL
- Disclosure text when commercial

Batch visual creation with an available image/design tool when explicitly requested. Do not publish automatically unless the user explicitly requests publishing and the necessary Pinterest connection is available. Before publishing, run this checklist:

1. Destination URL resolves and matches the promise.
2. Affiliate links comply with the affiliate program’s terms.
3. Pin and landing page disclosures are clear and conspicuous.
4. No deceptive claims, cloaked links, duplicate spam, or misleading before/after imagery.
5. Board choice is relevant and each pin is meaningfully fresh.

Current rules can change. Check Pinterest’s official affiliate and commercial-content guidance before asserting a requirement. Treat `#affiliate` or `#ad` as a practical disclosure option, not a substitute for required platform, FTC, or merchant disclosures.

### 7. Add the money layer

Use this prompt:

> Based on the article and pins above, suggest 3 realistic monetization options for this niche—one affiliate program, one digital product idea, one email-list lead magnet. Keep it specific to [niche], not generic.

Return exactly one option in each category, with fit, prerequisites, likely reader action, and the main compliance or operational risk. Do not promise earnings. Recommend checking program availability, commission terms, geographic eligibility, and disclosure rules before applying.

When the user asks for a broader monetization menu, evaluate these additional paths against the niche and audience instead of listing them generically:

- Affiliate marketing through approved programs such as Amazon Associates, ShareASale, LTK, or a niche-specific network. Confirm link, disclosure, and landing-page rules.
- Sponsored Pins or brand partnerships. Define the deliverable, audience fit, approval process, usage rights, payment, and paid-partnership disclosure before accepting.
- Shopping Ads or product catalog promotion for the user’s own store or an authorized merchant. Separate paid-ad costs from organic distribution and verify eligibility, catalog quality, and tracking.
- Digital products such as ebooks, courses, templates, printables, or checklists sold through the user’s site, Etsy, or Gumroad.
- Influencer or creator partnerships when the account has a credible audience and measurable niche engagement. Do not equate follower count with buying intent.
- Print-on-demand products such as shirts, mugs, wall art, or phone cases. Validate rights to every design and account for production, fulfillment, refunds, and margins.
- Dropshipping only when shipping times, product quality, returns, customer support, supplier reliability, and advertising economics are verified. Treat it as an ecommerce operation, not passive income.
- Blog display ads once traffic and page experience justify them. Do not prioritize ads so heavily that they damage readability or affiliate conversion.

Also recommend Pinterest Analytics for tracking impressions, saves, outbound clicks, closeups, audience signals, and conversions where available. Use those measurements to improve boards, creative, keywords, and offers. Consistency and genuine engagement can support reach and partnerships, but neither guarantees distribution or sponsorships.

### 8. Repeat weekly

Create a practical weekly production loop:

- Find one new or newly rising search in the same niche.
- Validate the keyword and choose a distinct content gap.
- Publish one useful article or refresh a genuinely outdated one.
- Create five fresh pins with varied promises and relevant boards.
- Review outbound clicks, saves, closeups, and conversions after enough data accumulates.

Use the user’s 80/20 helpful-to-promotional principle as a planning heuristic, not a guaranteed Pinterest ranking rule. Emphasize consistency, learning, and compounding evergreen distribution; never promise viral reach or passive income.

## Default final deliverable

When asked to run the full builder, return:

1. Campaign brief and assumptions
2. Trend evidence and keyword brief
3. Article package
4. Five-pin batch table
5. Five visual briefs
6. Publishing and disclosure checklist
7. Three monetization options
8. Weekly repeat plan

Clearly label estimates, user-supplied facts, current-source findings, and assumptions. Stop before publishing if a URL, disclosure, account connection, or compliance fact is missing.
