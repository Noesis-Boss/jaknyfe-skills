# The Decision Layer — Where Inputs Become Inputs (Ch. 4)

## The decision layer: capture is not a decision

A substrate full of captures is a holding pen, not a system. An item is not an input until it carries a label, a destination, and a next verb. "Competitor pricing PDF" is raw material. Tagged `ROUTE TO CLIENT / pricing conversation / due Friday` is an input. Externalizing a thought but keeping the loop is worse than before — now the thought lives in two places.

## The four bucket decisions (no fifth bucket — a fifth becomes where everything dies)

1. **Keep** — future value, no immediate action. Must earn a permanent home + retrieval path, and leave the active queue *today*. If you can't point to where it lives, you haven't kept it — you've relocated indecision.
2. **Act** — needs a verb from you on a timeline. The only bucket that earns space on tomorrow's task list. No clear verb + due date = noise wearing an urgent costume.
3. **Drop** — the moment passed. Not failure — triage. Log it (auditable) but treat with finality. Most-resisted bucket: we overvalue our own past captures.
4. **Route to Client** — belongs to someone you serve. Must leave your substrate today with a real handoff: what they do, by when, why it unblocks. No verb + date = you forwarded an open loop, not routed one.

## The two-touch rule

**Any item touched twice without a decision is a system leak.** Opened, scrolled past, re-read, paused on = a touch. Unfinished triage taxes everything else via attention residue. Fix: run one four-bucket pass — a coach cleared an 18-month, 180-entry backlog in 22 minutes (167 dropped, 7 kept, 6 routed) by finally making the deferred decisions.

## Decision debt compounds

Every deferred triage decision is debt. An item not triaged Monday sits next to Tuesday's captures, diluting both. By month's end the undecided pile is a permanent gray zone where items neither live nor die — they drag. **A substrate full of untriaged captures is worse than an empty one**: empty = zero drag; untriaged = *negative* drag, because each new item joins a pile you've trained yourself to avoid, until you stop capturing at all. The fix isn't a weekend cleanup (a lump-sum payoff) — it's **not accruing the debt**: daily, cheap, mechanical triage. The point isn't the size of any single payment; it's never carrying a balance.

## The nightly AI triage pass (the highest-use habit in the system)

One pass, run nightly. Four prompts, one per decision class — each names exactly ONE decision against ONE decision's criteria (never "organize my notes," which yields vague folders and confident noise):

| Prompt | Criterion it applies |
|---|---|
| **Keep** | Would I cite or return to this within 90 days? (domain relevance, reusable frameworks, recurring themes) |
| **Act** | Does this need a verb from me in the next 7 days? Output = a task list, not a folder. |
| **Drop** | Negation test: duplicates, stale impulses, expired context, fails "would I send this to a client / quote it in a proposal?" Log drops (silent drops breed regret; audited drops breed confidence). |
| **Route to Client** | Second-person signals — implied subject isn't you ("needs your legal review," "confirm Q3 scope before Friday"). Draft a one-line handoff; you approve. |

~12 minutes for ~60 captures/day. The model runs at the speed of *completion*, not deliberation — that mismatch is the whole benefit. You hand it the day's pile; you wake to **a queue, not a pile**. Morning job becomes execution only: approve handoffs, drag Act items onto today's list, start. The substrate stops being a burden and becomes a brief.

**Piles drain you. Queues direct you.**
