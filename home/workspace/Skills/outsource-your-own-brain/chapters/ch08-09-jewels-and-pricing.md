# Keep Only the Crown Jewels — and Price Your Outsourced Brain (Ch. 8–9)

Not everything deserves outsourcing. The failure pattern in both directions: delegating the crown jewels (client trust, taste, judgment) cheaply, and hoarding busywork out of guilt. Separate the two ruthlessly.

## The Crown Jewels test

**Question:** *Could a trained stranger do this to 80% of my quality without me in the room?*

- **Yes** → not a crown jewel; it's the setting (bench, velvet, security system). Delegable.
- **No** → crown jewel: your presence itself is the deliverable.

Typical crown jewels by trade:
- Consultant → diagnostic judgment, the uncomfortable truth session
- Writer → voice + selection of what's worth saying
- Coach → pattern recognition between sessions, the decision to push back
- Designer → editorial eye + stakeholder translation

Case: Marta listed 47 weekly tasks; 5 survived the filter (kickoff diagnostic, executive readout, scope negotiation, final yes, one monthly voice piece). "Being good at something is not the same as being the only person who can do it."

## Swiss cheese, not fortress

Delegation is not all-or-nothing. Don't build a fortress and hand over the keys. Carve several sized holes, each closable instantly if quality drops:

| Hole | Who takes it | You keep |
|---|---|---|
| Research + synthesis | AI assistant | Review the pack, cut half, refine |
| Drafting scaffolding | AI at stated voice | Sharpening, not blank-page starts |
| Scheduling, billing, intake, status | VA / PM | Escalation threshold only |
| Status meetings | Fractional PM | "Pull me in if the client is unhappy or we've slipped a date" |

Every hole needs a written checklist for what "done" looks like (length, format, sources, deadline) + a review step before the work touches a client. Two hours of procedures saves twenty hours of confused Slack.

Case: James (brand designer) carved three holes in one weekend → ~10 hrs/week reclaimed, clients reported *higher* responsiveness. Nadia (exec coach) delegated final report write-up → lost 2 of 3 anchor accounts within 6 months; the 90 minutes *was* the product.

## The never-delegate list

Five items that should not leave your hands, even when technically delegable:

1. **Client trust conversations** — frustration, confusion, churn risk, scope expansion. Delegating this delegates the relationship.
2. **Taste calls on final deliverables** — assistants produce options; you pick what ships. Final taste is your signature.
3. **Pricing and scope negotiation** — a delegate anchors lower, hedges harder, concedes sooner. Scope creep handled by anyone but you is scope creep you wear.
4. **The final yes on anything client-bound** — your eyes are the last gate. "The AI did it" is not a professional answer.
5. **Anything carrying your name directly** — speaking pitches, bios, thought leadership. Help with drafts/logistics is fine; substance carries your voice.

Test: *if this went wrong, would the client feel it as a breach of trust, not just quality?* Quality slips can be fixed. Trust slips end relationships. These five rarely exceed a quarter of your week — and they are the highest-value hours in the business.

## Crowning a trusted deputy

When capacity runs out even after Swiss-cheesing, crown one deputy for one or two crown jewels — rare and deliberate:
- You've worked together ≥ 1 year.
- You've documented how you make the decision — the *thinking*, not the output.
- Written promotion ladder with explicit limits.
- You keep the final yes until the deputy has held the jewel across several cycles without rescue.

The arc: crown jewels guarded → everything else Swiss-cheesed → deputy only for overflow, with a path back if the hole leaks.

## Pricing your outsourced brain (Ch. 9)

- **The death of billed hours** — price the outcome, not the process.
- **Productize the output, not the process.** The sales surface stays outcome-focused; the deliverable is priced for what it does, not how you made it.

**The use ratio** (know this number cold):

```
use ratio = revenue-per-deliverable ÷ (active supervision hours × pre-automation hourly rate)
```

Worked example: $4,200 deliverable, 20 min supervision, old rate $200/hr → $4,200 / ($200 × 0.33) = **63.6x**.

It tells you three things:
1. Which deliverables to double down on (high-use → more marketing/iteration)
2. Which clients to fire (low-use at high time-cost)
3. When to raise prices — **above 40x and you're not raising prices → you're subsidizing clients at your own expense**

Marcus's arc: $3,000/page at 2.75 hrs → 7.3x. Trusted the chain more, cut refinement to 1.5 hrs → 13.3x. Raised price to $4,500 on outcome → **20x**. Same chain.

Track weekly: price collected, active supervision minutes, pre-automation rate. Trend line = is the chain making you more, or just faster at the same money?

**Ethical pricing:** clients don't need to know your chain architecture; they do need to know AI is involved.
