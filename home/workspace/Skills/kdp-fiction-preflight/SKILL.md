# KDP Fiction Preflight

Review the supplied fiction manuscript and any accompanying front matter, back matter, blurb, metadata, links, publication notes, maps, illustrations, or series information as a conservative editorial and deliverable preflight. Identify the stated or inferred genre, but ask for clarification only when the choice would materially change the review. This is not legal advice, plagiarism certification, a substitute for developmental or copy editing, or an Amazon approval prediction.

## Operating rules

1. Read the complete supplied manuscript and supporting materials when feasible. If input is truncated, missing, image-only, or incomplete, state exactly what was not reviewed and reduce confidence.
2. Build a continuity ledger covering chapter/section, scene, POV character, tense, names, ages, physical markers, relationships, locations, timeline, world rules, promises, conflicts, clues, objects, and resolved/unresolved arcs.
3. Check for plagiarism-like passages, duplicate chapters/scenes, unfinished sections, placeholders, broken or suspicious links, unsupported quotations, copyright/trademark concerns, permissions issues, and AI-disclosure questions. Treat similarity as a review risk, never a definitive legal conclusion. Do not claim access to proprietary plagiarism databases unless one was actually used.
4. Check narrative logic and internal consistency. For speculative fiction, inspect world rules, technology, magic, creatures, history, and terminology. For historical fiction, flag claims or details that require factual verification without pretending to have verified them. For mystery and thriller, track clues, reveals, chronology, and fair-play resolution.
5. Distinguish verified findings from author confirmations. For AI disclosure, identify what the supplied materials show and what requires confirmation about the author's actual process and current KDP requirements.
6. Prefer minimally invasive revisions. Quote the smallest excerpt that proves the issue and propose a targeted correction rather than rewriting the manuscript wholesale.
7. Classify every finding as exactly one of: `Blocker`, `High`, `Medium`, or `Low`. Use `Blocker` only for a publication-stopping defect or unresolved legal, rights, safety, or compliance risk. If a category has no findings, say `None found in the supplied materials`.

## Required report

Use this structure:

# Amazon KDP Fiction Preflight Report

## Scope and confidence

State the files and materials reviewed, whether the manuscript was complete, the genre lens used, what was not checked, and important limitations. State that the report does not predict Amazon approval.

## Executive decision

Give one of: `Not ready`, `Conditionally ready after fixes`, or `Editorially ready for final production checks`. Summarize the top three risks and the reason for the decision.

## 1. Publish-blocking risks

Check plagiarism-like passages, unfinished sections, duplicate chapters/scenes, broken or suspicious links, placeholder text, unsupported quotations, attribution, copyright/trademark concerns, permissions, content warnings where relevant, and AI-disclosure questions.

## 2. Story readiness

Check plot continuity, character/name/age/physical-marker/location contradictions, timeline, POV and tense drift, pacing, scene purpose, setup and payoff, causal logic, world or setting consistency, clues and reveals where relevant, and unresolved arcs.

## 3. Line-edit findings

Check repeated wording, grammar, dialogue punctuation, paragraphing, sentence fragments, head-hopping, filter words, excessive adverbs, passive construction, awkward transitions, inconsistent capitalization or terminology, anachronistic wording, and unnatural or machine-like prose. Report representative patterns instead of flooding the report with trivial edits.

## 4. Genre and reader-market fit

Identify the genre lens used, then assess the opening hook, core reader promise, premise clarity, trope or convention delivery where relevant, pacing expectations, tone, content/heat level consistency where applicable, character or mystery payoff, emotional or thematic payoff, ending satisfaction, and series-readiness. Do not force romance criteria onto non-romance books. Frame this as editorial and market-readiness judgment, not a sales guarantee.

## 5. KDP deliverable checklist

Use a status table with `Pass`, `Needs review`, or `Missing` for front matter, title page, copyright page, table of contents where appropriate, content warnings where appropriate, maps/illustrations/permissions, dedication/epigraph attribution, back matter, links, blurb, author name, series data, edition/version data, author bio, cover-copy consistency, and AI-content disclosure status. Mark unverifiable items as `Needs review`, not `Pass`.

## Finding format

Every finding must include all fields below:

### [Severity] Short finding title

- **Location:** chapter/section, scene, page, paragraph, or artifact identifier when available
- **Excerpt:** exact manuscript excerpt, kept brief and verbatim; use `[excerpt unavailable]` only when the location is known but text cannot be quoted
- **Why it matters:** concrete reader, continuity, pacing, production, legal-risk, rights, or deliverable impact
- **Proposed revision:** minimally invasive action or replacement wording
- **Confidence:** High, Medium, or Low; explain uncertainty when below High

Do not omit a field. For a deliverable checklist row, use the same evidence convention in the notes column. Group findings only when they share a root cause, and name every affected chapter or artifact.

## Final action list

End with a prioritized list of no more than five actions, ordered by severity. Include a separate `Author confirmations needed` subsection for rights ownership, permissions, genre/category decisions, pen name/legal name decisions, series metadata, historical or scientific fact checks, content warnings, and AI use/disclosure.

## Quality control before responding

- Verify every finding has a severity, location, exact excerpt, rationale, and minimally invasive revision.
- Verify chapter, scene, page, and quotation references against supplied materials.
- Do not claim plagiarism, copyright infringement, factual verification, or Amazon approval without evidence.
- Keep genre-specific editorial judgments separate from universal publication blockers.
- State what was not checked because the necessary material or external source was absent.
