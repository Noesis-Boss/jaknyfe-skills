# Your Design DNA

You are about to run a taste interview. The goal is not to design anything today. The goal is to discover what this person's eye already knows, put words to it, and save it as a permanent skill so every future design Zo makes for them carries their signature.

This works for two kinds of people. Adapt to whichever one you are talking to:

- **The person with no design vocabulary.** They know what they like when they see it but cannot name it. Never ask them for design terms. Ask for feelings, places, objects, and reactions. You translate.
- **The person with strong opinions.** They already have references and hills they will die on. Move faster, go deeper, and capture their exact words.

## How to run the interview

Ask one question at a time. Wait for the answer before moving on. Keep it warm and conversational, like a creative director getting to know a new client over coffee. Never make it feel like a form.

### 1. Love

Ask them to share 2 or 3 things they find beautiful. Websites, photos, a hotel lobby, an album cover, a place. Screenshots and links are ideal but a description works. For each one, ask the follow-up that matters most: "what is the thing about this one that gets you?"

### 2. Repel

Ask for 1 or 2 things that make them close the tab. What they hate is often more revealing than what they love. Listen for the pattern: is it clutter, loudness, coldness, sameness?

### 3. Feeling words

Ask: "When someone lands on something you made, what are the 2 or 3 words you want them to feel?" Push past generic answers. If they say "clean," ask "clean like a hospital or clean like a Kyoto ryokan?"

### 4. Palette instinct

Do not ask for hex codes. Ask instinct questions: warm or cool? Soft and atmospheric or bold and graphic? More like dawn, dusk, midday, or midnight? An ocean, a forest, a desert, or a city? From their answers, propose one small palette (4 or 5 colors with hex codes) and show it to them described in plain words. Let them react and adjust.

### 5. Typography personality

Ask: "If your project were a person speaking, what is the voice? A calm editor, a warm host, a sharp founder, a playful friend?" Translate their answer into a type direction (serif or sans, weight, spacing, editorial or utilitarian) and say it back in plain language.

### 6. Signature move

Every distinctive designer has one recurring move. A gradient they always reach for, generous whitespace, full-bleed photography, a numbered index, tiny footnotes. Ask: "Is there one visual thing you find yourself wanting again and again?" If they do not know, propose one based on everything above.

## What to produce

When the interview is done, write a new skill at `Skills/my-design-dna/SKILL.md` in their workspace using the template in `assets/generated-skill-template.md`. Fill every section with their actual answers and their actual words. Quote them where their phrasing was vivid. Do not water it down into generic design language; specificity is the entire point.

The generated skill must include the "How to work with me" section from the template. That section teaches Zo the iteration ritual: show a first version fast, then refine in named passes, and how this person tends to give feedback.

After writing it, tell them plainly: "This is your design DNA. From now on, any time I build something visual for you, I will read this first. Tell me any time a preference changes and I will update it."

## Rules for the interviewer

- One question per message. Never dump the whole questionnaire.
- Mirror their language back. If they say "dreamy," the skill says "dreamy," not "ethereal aesthetic."
- If they share images or links, actually study them before responding.
- Strong opinions are welcome. If their answers conflict (they love minimal but also love maximal collage), name the tension and ask which one is home and which one is a vacation.
- The interview should take 10 to 15 minutes. If they are flying, go faster. Never pad it.
