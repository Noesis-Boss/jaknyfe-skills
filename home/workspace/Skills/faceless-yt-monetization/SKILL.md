# faceless-yt-monetization

Use this skill as **stage 5** of the faceless-youtube-system. Stages: niche-brand → content-engine → discoverability → retention-script → monetization.

This stage runs **in parallel with stages 2–4**, not strictly after them. A 6-month roadmap depends on a real calendar, but the calendar is still being built; this stage produces the framework and updates as content lands.

## Required input

- **Niche + audience + pillars** from `Skills/faceless-yt-niche-brand/`.
- **Content calendar** from `Skills/faceless-yt-content-engine/` (the planned content mix).
- **Channel state** — start state is "zero subscribers." If re-running, pass current subscriber count, view velocity, and revenue.

## Output (single markdown document)

### Section 1 — Realistic 6-month roadmap (3 stages)

For each stage: priorities, metrics, video test counts, improvement triggers, and the explicit "if X, then Y" branching that prevents the plan from lying to the creator about progress.

#### Month 1 — Foundation (zero → first signal)

- **Priorities**: 8 videos shipped (2/week) using the 30-day calendar; packaging (CTR) and watch time are the two metrics the algorithm actually rewards in month 1. Skip monetization setup; the channel is not eligible yet and optimizing for it now creates wrong incentives.
- **Metrics to track**:
  - Impressions (channel-level target: 10K/week by week 4).
  - CTR (target band: 2–10%; <2% means packaging failed, >10% means title/thumbnail are gaming).
  - Average view duration (AVD) as % of video length (target: >40% for 8–12 min videos; <30% means the script dropped viewers).
  - Subscriber conversion rate (subscribers gained per 1,000 views; target band: 5–20 — high is great, low at month 1 is normal).
- **Test count**: 8 videos is the test. Do not redesign packaging or script mid-month; you do not have data to redesign from.
- **Improvement triggers**:
  - CTR <2% on ≥3 of 8 videos → packaging problem; revisit `Skills/faceless-yt-discoverability/` and re-do title/thumbnail for the next 2 videos only.
  - AVD <30% on ≥3 of 8 videos → script problem; revisit `Skills/faceless-yt-retention-script/` and add a payoff moment to the first 60s.
  - CTR high but AVD low → packaging oversold the video. Do not retitle — the title worked. Fix the script.
- **Realistic outcome band**: 0–1,000 subscribers, 0–10K total views. Anything above this band is a strong month; below is normal.

#### Months 2–3 — Validation (first signal → repeatable signal)

- **Priorities**: 16 more videos (2/week). Begin 1–2 A/B tests on thumbnail text per week. Begin tracking returning-viewer % (the metric that decides whether the channel has a real audience or just impressions).
- **Metrics to track**:
  - Returning viewers per video (target: 20%+ of viewership is returning for 8-min+ videos; <10% means the channel has no audience, only viewers).
  - Subscriber velocity (subs/week; if it's flat, the channel is not converting view-to-sub).
  - Comments per video (target band: 5–50 for a new faceless channel; sparser than face-cam because there's no personality to react to).
- **Test count**: 8 videos is still the test band; do not let the channel count exceed 24 videos before deciding whether the niche works.
- **Improvement triggers**:
  - Subs flat after 16 videos → the value proposition is unclear or the niche is too saturated for a new channel. Re-run `Skills/faceless-yt-niche-brand/` for a tighter angle; do not keep grinding the same angle.
  - Returning viewers <10% → the content does not reward loyalty. Add a recurring segment, a series, or a "Part 2" hook to the next 4 videos.
  - Comments low (<3/video average) → the opinion/debate pieces from the calendar are not landing; swap them for explicit question-style CTAs at the end of videos.
- **Realistic outcome band**: 100–5,000 subscribers, 10K–100K total views. Apply for YPP (YouTube Partner Program) when 1,000 subs + 4,000 watch hours are crossed.

#### Months 4–6 — Compounding (repeatable signal → first dollars)

- **Priorities**: 24 more videos (2/week). Apply to YPP the moment eligibility hits. Begin testing 1 non-ad monetization path (Section 3) on a single video per month; do not stack paths yet.
- **Metrics to track**:
  - RPM (revenue per mille, per 1,000 views) — varies wildly by niche ($1–$30); track the channel's own baseline before comparing to public numbers.
  - Revenue per video from each non-ad source (track each path separately; do not lump into "sponsorship").
  - Subscriber-to-view ratio (the percentage of viewers who subscribe per video; the long-term compounding metric).
- **Test count**: This is the period where each video is its own A/B; do not redesign the channel. Improve one variable at a time and let the data decide.
- **Improvement triggers**:
  - RPM below the niche's lower band → the audience is wrong or the content is unmonetizable. Consider moving to a more lucrative sub-niche within the same umbrella.
  - Non-ad path showing <1% click-through after 3 videos → the path is not working; do not run it again. Pick a different path.
  - Subscriber velocity stalled → the channel has hit the niche's organic ceiling; either expand the niche, add Shorts, or begin cross-posting.
- **Realistic outcome band**: 500–20,000 subscribers, 100K–1M total views, $0–$2,000/month from a mix of ads + 1 non-ad path. Anything above is strong; below is normal for a 6-month-old faceless channel.

### Section 2 — Milestones (the explicit "am I on track?" markers)

A clean list the creator can tick off:

- 8 videos shipped.
- 1 video with >1,000 views.
- 1 video with >50% AVD.
- 100 subscribers.
- 1,000 subscribers + 4,000 watch hours (YPP eligibility).
- First YPP payment.
- First $100 from a non-ad path.
- First $1,000/month from a single non-ad path.

List these in the order they will be crossed, not by importance. The order is the creator's actual progress indicator.

### Section 3 — 3 realistic non-ad monetization paths

Each path: what it is, the niche-fit check (must reference the niche from stage 1, not be generic), the minimum audience size to attempt it, the realistic revenue band at month 6, and the failure mode.

Recommended coverage (pick 3 from these, matched to the niche):

1. **Affiliate** (lowest bar, slowest compounding). Niche-fit check: the niche has real products the viewer would buy anyway.
2. **Sponsorship / sponsorship category** (highest revenue per video, hardest to land). Niche-fit check: the niche has a small number of B2B-style vendors with YouTube ad budgets.
3. **Digital product** (template, guide, course, paid community). Niche-fit check: the niche's viewer has a recurring need the channel's content addresses but cannot fully solve in 10 minutes.
4. **Service / consulting** (only for creator-led faceless channels where the channel is a marketing surface for the creator's paid work).
5. **Membership / community** (Patreon-style). Niche-fit check: the niche's viewer has an identity to belong to (hobby, profession, recovery, fandom).

Do not recommend a path that does not fit the niche. Affiliate is a default for some niches and a mistake for others.

### Section 4 — 2 simple digital-product ideas

For each:

- **Product name + format** (template pack, 30-page guide, mini-course, Notion workspace, etc.).
- **What problem it solves** (must be a problem the channel's videos already prove the viewer has).
- **Why the channel's audience is the right buyer** (the audience-fit argument).
- **Production effort band** (low / medium / high).
- **Realistic price band** (in USD).
- **First-sale trigger** (the specific content moment in the next 60 days that would seed the first sale).

### Section 5 — Sponsorship outreach template

A reusable cold-outreach email to a potential sponsor, with placeholders for:

- Channel name + niche.
- Top 3 videos by view count + watch time (the metrics a sponsor actually looks at).
- Audience description (the persona from stage 1, condensed).
- What the sponsor gets (integration type, length, frequency).
- A one-line ask (pilot video, paid trial, or sample integration).

Keep the email ≤ 150 words. Sponsors ignore long emails. Do not include a media kit PDF unless asked; the email itself is the pitch.

### Section 6 — Improvement rules across all stages

- **One variable at a time.** A/B title or thumbnail, not both, on the same video.
- **Let the data decide for 8 videos minimum.** A single viral video is not a signal.
- **No algorithm tricks in month 1.** Sub4Sub, clickbait, and engagement-bait destroy the metrics that matter by month 3.
- **Compare to your own baseline, not public numbers.** Public "average RPM" numbers ignore niche, audience, and watch-time distribution.

## Quality rules

- **No income promises.** Every band is a band, not a target. A sustainable channel is one the creator will still be running in 12 months, not one that paid $5K in month 4.
- **No "post daily" advice.** 2/week is the default; the niche and the creator's capacity may justify different frequency, but daily is rarely the right answer for a faceless channel.
- **No platform-coupling.** The roadmap should not depend on a single platform feature (YPP, Shorts, etc.) the creator cannot control.
- **No get-rich-quick framing.** "Sustainable" means the channel keeps producing when the creator is tired, sick, or busy. If the plan requires grinding 7 days a week to hit $1K, it is not sustainable.
