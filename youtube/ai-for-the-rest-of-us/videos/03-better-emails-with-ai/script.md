# Script — How to Write Better Emails With AI—Without Sounding Robotic

**Format:** Tuesday tutorial. Cold open on camera, then 4 numbered steps with screen demos and voice-over, brief on-camera safety minute, on-camera outro.
**Target:** 6 minutes (~1,450 words at tested narration pace).
**Tone:** Don's established voice — peer, not guru. Jargon-free. Prompts typed live on screen.

---

**[COLD OPEN — on camera]**

"I hope this email finds you well." If AI wrote that for you, it didn't find you well — it found you generic.

Here's the thing nobody tells you: the problem isn't AI. It's the instructions we give it. Four steps — free, about five minutes — and it stops sounding like a robot wrote it.

**[INTRO — VO over title card]**

I'm Don. I've written work email for over forty years, and this channel, AI for the Rest of Us, is where I test AI tools before you waste an afternoon on them. Tuesday is tutorial day: one problem, solved.

The problem isn't that AI can't write. It's that we give it instructions like a mood, not a job. "Make it professional." That's a mood. Jobs get results. Let me show you.

**[STEP 1 — Give it a job, not a vibe]**

Step one: give the AI a job, not a vibe.

"Make this professional" tells the AI how to feel, not what to do. And it guesses. Here's the fix — describe the job like you'd describe it to a new assistant. Who's it for? What do they need from it? What happens after they read it?

Watch. I'm typing: "This email goes to a client who's frustrated about a missed deadline. They need a clear apology, the actual fix, and a date. Keep it under 120 words."

That's a job. Notice what I didn't say — "professional." The job description handled it. A frustrated client plus a clear fix plus a date IS professional, and AI fills in the details you didn't have to specify.

**[STEP 2 — The four-word fix]**

Step two — and this is the one I promised at the start. Four words that fix a robotic draft: "Make it sound human."

That's it. Type the four words after any draft that reads like a form letter. The difference is immediate. It drops the stiff openers, kills "furthermore," and writes like a person who has somewhere to be.

Here's why this works: AI defaults to formal because formal is safe. "Make it sound human" gives it permission to write the way your best email actually reads — the one you dashed off in ninety seconds and got a reply to in ten minutes.

If "human" still isn't quite right, try "make it sound like a text message from a smart friend." Same idea, more pressure.

**[STEP 3 — Sound like you]**

Step three: make it sound like you.

Here's the test. Would your closest coworker know you wrote this, or would they suspect a ghostwriter? If AI wrote it, they'd suspect. Every AI email has the same fingerprints — and your colleagues are starting to recognize them.

The fix takes two minutes. Copy three or four of your own emails from your Sent folder — the ones you're proud of, or honestly, just the ones that got quick replies. Paste them into the AI and type: "These are my emails. Match my style — my rhythm, my greetings, how I sign off. Match it to this new email."

Now it's not writing like AI. It's writing like you on a good day — before coffee. That's the goal. Not better than you. You, with the rough edges filed off.

**[STEP 4 — The six tells]**

Step four: know the six tells that scream "AI wrote this."

Even good drafts slip these in. Quick list — check your next one against it.

One: "I hope this email finds you well." Nobody writes that to someone they actually know. Two: "delve" — AI loves that word. Humans say "dig into." Three: "It's important to note" — if it's important, just say the thing. Four: em-dash overload. We all like an em-dash. AI writes six in one paragraph. Five: "Additionally," starting three paragraphs in a row. Six: a summary paragraph that restates everything you just read, ending in "Thank you for your understanding." If nothing needed understanding, cut it.

Find one of those, delete it, and watch the email get instantly more human.

**[SAFETY MINUTE — on camera]**

The safety minute. AI is for tone and structure — never for facts. It will invent a date, a price, or a name and state it like gospel. Verify every number and every name before you hit send. And never paste private information — salaries, account numbers, anything confidential — into a chatbot. Tone help in. Facts checked out.

**[OUTRO — on camera]**

So: a job, not a vibe. "Make it sound human." Match your Sent folder. And kill the six tells. That's the whole method.

Your homework: take one email you've been avoiding — the hard one — and run it through these four steps. Tell me in the comments which of the six tells you see most in your inbox. I read every comment.

If this saved you an email you were dreading, the like button helps other beginners find the channel. Next Friday I'm testing the big three — ChatGPT against Claude against Gemini — to find out which one is actually easiest for beginners. See you then.
