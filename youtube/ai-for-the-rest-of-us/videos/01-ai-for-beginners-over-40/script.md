**\[SCREEN DEMO + VO — 2:00\]**

First real question. Don't ask it something silly like "what is AI" — ask it something you actually need this week. Watch: I'm going to type, "I need to politely decline a meeting invitation that conflicts with a doctor's appointment. Write a short professional email." Enter.

**\[SCREEN DEMO + VO — 3:30\]**

Let me give you your first five words of AI vocabulary. That's genuinely all you need to follow any conversation about this stuff.

One: **prompt** — that's just the instructions you type. "Write me an email" is a prompt. You've already been doing it.

Two: **chatbot** — the whole assistant, like ChatGPT. You've used one. Done.

Three: **hallucination** — when the AI confidently states something that's flat wrong. It's not lying, exactly. It's predicting plausible words. This is why we always double-check anything that matters.

Four: **model** — the engine under the hood. You'll hear "GPT" or "Claude" or "Gemini." Different engines, same idea, like different car brands.

Five: **token** — you'll see this on pricing pages. It just means chunk-of-text. When a pricing page says "tokens," read "words-ish." That's the whole mystery.

If you know those five, you know more than most people who talk about AI at dinner parties.

**\[SCREEN DEMO + VO — 5:30\]**

Now the part that matters for everyday life. Here are three real things I'd do with AI this week — all on the free tier.

First, email. You saw it already. But try this: paste in a confusing email you received and ask, "Explain what this person is asking me to do, in plain English." It's a decoder ring for corporate-speak.

Second, planning. I'm going to ask it to help plan something real: "I'm hosting eight people for dinner Saturday. Make me a shopping list and a prep timeline, starting Thursday." Look — it gave me a timeline, a list, even a reminder to check for dietary restrictions, which honestly is more than I would have remembered.

Third — and this is the one people your age are using it for most — learning. "Explain the stock market's price-to-earnings ratio like I'm smart but never studied finance." Or anything else you've been curious about. It's the most patient teacher that has ever existed. It never sighs, never makes you feel dumb for asking twice.

**\[SCREEN DEMO + VO — 7:30\]**

Now, the safety minute. This is important, so I'm going to say it plainly.

AI gets things wrong — confidently, fluently, with total conviction. It will make up a book title, a statistic, even a web address, and it will look exactly like the truth. So here's the rule I live by: **if it matters, verify it.** Medical question — ask your doctor, use AI to prepare better questions. Financial decision — verify with the actual source. Anything with a citation — click the citation yourself and see if it's real.

Two more quick things. Never paste into any AI something you wouldn't want on a postcard: passwords, Social Security numbers, client confidences. Free chatbots may use your conversations for training, so treat them accordingly. And be very aware that AI-powered scams are getting good — voice cloning, flawless phishing emails. A little skepticism is now a life skill. If an urgent message asks for money or a code, hang up and call the person back on the number you already have.
